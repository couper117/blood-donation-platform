Health routines, advice and treatment

General guidance for four common conditions. Pick a condition to read its routine, advice and usual treatment.

This information is general and educational only. It is not a substitute for professional medical advice. Always speak to a doctor about your own treatment.

Diabetes

High blood sugar caused by problems with insulin.

Daily routine

  • Check blood sugar as advised by your doctor.
  • Eat meals at regular times, avoid skipping meals.
  • Aim for about 30 minutes of walking or activity.
  • Take medication at the same time each day.

Advice

  • Reduce sugary drinks, sweets and white bread.
  • Choose whole grains, vegetables and lean protein.
  • Care for your feet and check for cuts.
  • Keep regular eye and kidney check-ups.

Usual treatment

  • Lifestyle and diet changes first.
  • Tablets such as metformin (as prescribed).
  • Insulin injections for some patients.
  • Regular monitoring of HbA1c levels.

Obesity

Excess body fat that can raise the risk of other illnesses.

Daily routine

  • Plan balanced meals and controlled portions.
  • Be active for 30 to 60 minutes most days.
  • Drink water instead of sugary drinks.
  • Get regular, good quality sleep.

Advice

  • Set small, realistic weight goals.
  • Eat more vegetables, fruit and fibre.
  • Limit fried food, fast food and snacks.
  • Track your progress and stay consistent.

Usual treatment

  • Diet and exercise programme with support.
  • Advice from a dietitian.
  • Medication in some cases (as prescribed).
  • Surgery only for severe cases, decided by doctors.

Asthma

A condition where the airways narrow and make breathing hard.

Daily routine

  • Take your preventer inhaler as prescribed.
  • Keep your reliever inhaler with you.
  • Warm up before exercise.
  • Follow your written asthma action plan.

Advice

  • Avoid triggers like smoke, dust and pollen.
  • Keep your home clean and well aired.
  • Get the flu vaccine if advised.
  • Learn the signs of an attack early.

Usual treatment

  • Reliever inhalers (e.g. salbutamol) for symptoms.
  • Preventer inhalers (steroids) taken daily.
  • Spacer devices to help inhaler use.
  • Emergency care for severe attacks.

Hypertension (high blood pressure)

Blood pressure that stays higher than the healthy range.

Daily routine

  • Take blood pressure medicine at the same time daily.
  • Check your blood pressure regularly.
  • Do light exercise such as walking.
  • Practise relaxation to manage stress.

Advice

  • Reduce salt in your food.
  • Eat more fruit and vegetables.
  • Limit alcohol and stop smoking.
  • Keep a healthy weight.

Usual treatment

  • Lifestyle changes first.
  • Medicines such as ACE inhibitors or diuretics.
  • Regular blood pressure monitoring.
  • Treating other risks like high cholesterol.

Anemia

Not enough healthy red blood cells to carry oxygen around the body - the condition most directly linked to blood donation and iron levels.

Daily routine

  • Take iron or vitamin supplements exactly as prescribed.
  • Pair iron-rich meals with vitamin C to help absorption.
  • Rest when tired rather than pushing through fatigue.
  • Track how you feel - dizziness or breathlessness is worth reporting.

Advice

  • Eat iron-rich foods: beans, leafy greens, red meat, fortified cereals.
  • Avoid donating blood again until you are confirmed eligible.
  • Tea and coffee around meals can reduce iron absorption - space them out.
  • See the Medicines page for iron, folic acid and B12 supplements.

Usual treatment

  • Iron supplements for iron-deficiency anaemia.
  • Folic acid or vitamin B12 for other types.
  • Treating the underlying cause (e.g. heavy bleeding).
  • Blood transfusion only for severe cases, decided by a doctor.

Heart health

Keeping the heart and blood vessels healthy - closely linked to blood pressure, cholesterol and weight.

Daily routine

  • Aim for at least 30 minutes of activity most days.
  • Take heart-related medication at the same time each day.
  • Avoid smoking and limit alcohol.
  • Manage stress with rest, sleep and relaxation.

Advice

  • Follow a heart-healthy diet: more vegetables, whole grains and fish.
  • Reduce salt, sugar and saturated fat.
  • Keep a healthy weight and waist size.
  • Know your blood pressure and cholesterol numbers.

Usual treatment

  • Lifestyle changes are the first step for most people.
  • Medicines such as statins or blood-pressure tablets as prescribed.
  • Regular check-ups to monitor heart health.
  • Emergency care immediately for chest pain or heart-attack signs.
